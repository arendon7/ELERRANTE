# EL ERRANTE · V4 Copy Principles

## Public surface rule

The public site should create appetite and curiosity before it explains methodology.

The current editorial depth remains valuable, but Home must stop behaving like a long essay before the visitor has chosen to care.

## Voice

Write as a cook with criteria, not as an advertising agency describing a restaurant.

Prefer:

- concrete culinary nouns and verbs;
- short statements;
- material consequences;
- confident but bounded claims;
- rhythm and contrast.

Avoid:

- luxury adjectives used as substitutes for evidence;
- generic hospitality promises;
- decorative Italian language;
- excessive manifesto tone;
- repeating the same idea in headline, paragraph and CTA.

## Useful vocabulary

`masa`, `estructura`, `fermentación`, `fuego`, `producto`, `humedad`, `acidez`, `grasa`, `tostado`, `textura`, `madurez`, `ritmo`, `territorio`, `prueba`, `decisión`, `segundo fuego`, `movimiento`.

## Headline test

A strong headline should do at least one of these:

1. create desire;
2. state a point of view;
3. explain a product difference;
4. invite a next action.

If it only sounds elegant, rewrite it.

## CTA test

CTA labels describe destination/action, not mood.

Prefer:

- `Ver las pizzas`
- `Pedir En Casa`
- `Cotizar un evento`
- `Conocer el método`
- `Leer la bitácora`

Avoid vague labels such as:

- `Descubre más`
- `Vive la experiencia`
- `Conoce más`

unless the destination truly cannot be named more precisely.

## Narrative compression

When moving V3 content into V4:

- keep the underlying argument;
- remove repeated setup;
- keep one memorable sentence on Home;
- move proof/explanation to the relevant deep page;
- preserve technical caveats where a short claim could become misleading.

## Colombia

Colombia enters through ingredients, decisions, producers, climate, interpretation and authorship—not flags, folklore or forced national symbolism.

## Italy

Italy is school/reference. Do not stage Italy and Colombia as rivals or use Italian authenticity as the primary value proposition.
