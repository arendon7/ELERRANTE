# El Errante Brand V4

Use this skill before any public-facing design or content change in El Errante.

## Canon source

Read `documentacion/V4_BRAND_DIRECTION.md` first. It is the binding V4 visual and UX canon.

When relevant, also read:

- `documentacion/CANON_EDITORIAL_V30.md` for culinary voice, evidence and claims.
- `documentacion/CANON_VISUAL_PRODUCTO_V305.md` for product-image authenticity and roles.
- `assets/images/brand-final/manifest-hq-v13.json` only as the legacy image inventory; do not treat its art direction as V4 canon.

## Non-negotiable identity

- Brand: `EL ERRANTE`.
- Descriptor: `PIZZA CONTEMPORÁNEA`.
- Year: `EST. 2019`.
- Canonical emblem: approved walking pizzaiolo tossing dough.
- Dominant palette: carbon black / warm black / ivory.
- Gold is restrained accent, never dominant decoration.
- Terracotta is no longer the institutional primary color.
- The old terracotta E mark is legacy and must not be reintroduced.
- Never reconstruct the master logo with HTML text or substitute typography.

## Brand feeling

The public experience must feel dark, elegant, cinematic, editorial, tactile, warm, precise, alive, travelled and contemporary.

Use the Johnnie Walker reference only as a strategic analogy: heritage + movement + ritual + strong emblem + product-led storytelling. Never copy its trade dress, layouts, bottle language or distinctive graphic assets.

## Culinary truth

Italy is school; Colombia is the territory of interpretation.

Show craft through decisions and product, not generic luxury language. Preserve the culinary principles: light structured dough, controlled fermentation, toppings composed as recipes, excellent product, texture contrast and a distinct identity.

## Surface hierarchy

1. Desire.
2. Identity.
3. Product.
4. Movement/hospitality.
5. Method.
6. Authorship.
7. Evidence.

Use the operating principle: the work first, author after, method underneath, evidence always.

## Design rules

- Prefer editorial sequences over repetitive card grids.
- Use large photography, asymmetry, breathing space and deliberate scale changes.
- Keep surface copy short; move depth to Method, History, Product and Bitácora.
- Avoid SaaS icon tiles, excessive rounded cards, generic beige UI, decorative gradients and cliché Italian styling.
- Product photography must dominate product surfaces.
- Each approved image needs one primary canonical narrative role.
- Generated images with malformed logos, text, packaging, anatomy or product truth are never publishable.
- Build mobile as an intentional composition, not a compressed desktop page.

## Motion rules

Movement supports the wandering emblem and narrative, but restraint is mandatory.

- Animate hierarchy or state change, not everything.
- Prefer short transforms/opacity transitions.
- Avoid permanent floating, looping decoration and theatrical scroll hijacking.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The page must remain complete and beautiful with motion disabled.

## Copy rules

Voice: cultured but not academic, technical but not cold, sensory but not baroque, confident without grandiosity, Colombian without folklore.

Avoid using as primary claims: `premium`, `gourmet`, `auténtico`, `exclusivo`, `único`, `hecho con pasión`, `ingredientes de la más alta calidad`.

Do not publish operational or technical assertions unless supported by current canonical evidence.

## Workflow

For every substantial public redesign:

1. Audit current implementation and identify legacy V1/V3 visual debt.
2. State the page's single primary job and conversion path.
3. Inventory existing assets by role and truth status.
4. Design the information hierarchy before styling details.
5. Apply V4 tokens and master logo assets.
6. Use the strongest approved imagery; do not fill space with duplicates.
7. Review desktop and mobile independently.
8. Audit accessibility, keyboard behavior, motion and performance.
9. Run functional regression before promotion.
10. Never merge a visual change that degrades store, checkout, operations or internal modules.

## Reject a design when

- it could belong to any artisan pizzeria;
- it becomes rustic-Italian décor;
- gold becomes flashy or ornamental;
- terracotta again becomes dominant;
- the page is a stack of cards;
- typography lacks editorial contrast;
- photography is repetitive or filler;
- copy explains too much before creating desire;
- the old E logo returns;
- a generated visual is presented as documentary evidence.
